Introduction: using the part and motion toolbox

Current observation setup: only agentview and wrist RGB-D, shared by all tools.
Inspect their full-resolution originals. Choose one view where the intended
contact face is visible and mark its original pixels; the other view gives
context. Wrist calibration belongs to that capture, since the camera moves.
Use positive joint_degrees with rotation_direction='cw' or 'ccw' for hinge motion.
View from the returned positive hinge-axis end toward its origin: CCW is positive.
The largest-magnitude world component of the hinge axis is positive (exact ties:
X,Y,Z). A near-vertical hinge therefore points up; a horizontal hinge points
toward +X or +Y. Point order and image arrows do not change that convention.
It changes sign only, never the fitted hinge line, and does not verify opening/closing.
Use the preview's contact_effect to distinguish a push into the selected face
from motion away from it. To push from the other side, observe and select that
actual face; changing a normal sign does not create contact on a hidden surface.

You choose the task strategy, evidence to collect, experiments, recovery and
lessons to retain. The tools provide measurements and numerical calculations.
Start from the current task instruction, public observations and episode budget.
Keep the task goal distinct from a proposed motion. Identify the desired
relationship between the moving part and a fixed reference. A camera-facing
normal, a hinge annotation, or a successful hand stroke does not determine
whether that relationship improved. Grounding reports your geometric hypothesis;
it does not verify that a positive angle achieves the task. Inspect paired fixed-
camera feedback after uncertain actions before treating visible motion as progress.
Use compact replies and useful images; load detailed help or saved arrays when
needed. Depth/calibration, IK and collision calculations belong in numerical tools.

The perception tools connect through returned IDs:
1. segment_parts: select a visible part with a coarse box or clicks; inspect
   the proposed masks and choose a part_id. No hinge annotation is required.
2. associate_views: optionally examine that observed surface in other cameras
   from the same capture. Occlusion and disagreement are different outcomes.
   For grounding, pass a current selected part_id as ground's moving_part_id
   or contact_part_id in the same camera. This reuses the mask, including its
   holes. Omit regions.contact_box_xyxy when only door/joint geometry is needed.
   A failed handle fit can still return a door-only model; inspect handle_fit.
   push and hook_pull reuse the same moving_part_id/contact_part_id and masks.
   With a selected part ID, region_xyxy can be omitted. Surface contact selection
   also accepts contact_part_id. Reuse observed parts instead of reselecting boxes.
3. track_part_motion: follow the selected part through saved observations.
   A clear reference can come from any included frame; keep observations ordered.
   For live agentview recordings, track_video_motion uses extra RGB frames while
   keeping measured depth at the supplied observation timestamps. Other cameras
   use track_part_motion. More frames are not a guarantee of a better estimate.
4. estimate_and_verify_joint: fit a joint hypothesis from measured motion and
   optionally test it against a later motion interval. An unverified estimate
   and a motion-supported estimate are different results.

5. apply_joint_estimate: explicitly install a current estimate by ID, then obtain
   fresh plans. The canonical axis convention and current contact geometry remain.
   Without a grounded handle, provide current_part_id
   to build a model from the current observed part for joint-linked pushing.
   A supported joint does not require a vertical handle or establish a pinch.
   A supported unsigned axis fit does not verify opening/closing direction.
   After a pivot correction, check that your signed motion still serves the task.
   Use refresh_contact for current handle/panel geometry when a fixed-world joint
   hypothesis remains useful; it does not replace that hinge with another visual
   guess. apply_joint_estimate(..., reuse_fixed_joint=true) explicitly reuses an
   older estimate from this episode under the fixed-world-joint assumption. It
   updates the joint parameter, not the current part pose or contact evidence.

Start with the reset camera_overview: it shows already captured cameras in one
sheet. Open an original view where the intended contact and attachment edge are
visible before marking pixels. The free edge of a moving panel is not its hinge.
Use the other available view to inspect your selected geometry when unsure. A failed
contact fit does not install a different vertical feature as a handle; select
the intended part yourself. A motion_effect reporting a stationary region while
the hand moved calls for checking part identity/contact before further motion.
Unknown object motion is not automatically replaced with an attached-hand guess.
If tracking measured motion but the automatic scene update was unresolved,
apply_part_motion transports the previous observed part/contact model. Choose
a reference capture matching scene_reference_control_step and a current capture.
After a contact skill invalidates the active scene, its retained reference step
can anchor this measured update. Reusing a fixed joint preserves its canonical
axis convention; it supplies no new current pose by itself.
If current geometry must be refitted instead, refresh_contact keeps the joint.
Watch grasp_evidence: measured pad gap comes from robot proprioception. If it
collapses toward fully closed during manipulation, recheck contact rather than
assuming the close command established a grasp. This is not a tactile certificate.
   Unsupported checking motion requires an explicitly provisional hypothesis;
   a candidate contradicted by its checking interval is rejected by this tool.

The perception calls issue no motion and do not update the planning model.
They need observed object movement to estimate an axis; they cannot create a
successful probe or substitute gripper movement for object movement. You choose
whether and how to gather more evidence using the available action tools.
Planning returns predictions; use captures returned after executed actions as
motion evidence.

In live work, treat a visually marked hinge as an initial hypothesis. When the
hinge is uncertain or predicted movement disagrees with observations, prefer
measured part motion to repeated visual guesses. After informative movement,
check the estimate and explicitly apply it if useful before further planning.
Stop on task success; do not collect extra motion solely to exercise a tool.

When a result is unsupported, inspect its reason, support measurements and
overlays. Consider a different view, part selection, reference or informative
motion; decide based on the evidence and remaining budget. Report unresolved
when evidence remains inadequate. Repeating the same request adds no observations.

Current scope: upright revolute joints. The unsigned axis does not determine
the requested task direction. Mask scores and geometric residuals are not
calibrated correctness probabilities. Offline tracking uses the supplied clip;
later-interval agreement is not independent future confirmation.

Before planning, read the relevant grounding/model-update contract: an axis
alone is not a contact or collision model. Plans do not execute themselves;
use fresh plans after model changes. Check observed outcomes after actions;
a successful fit or plan does not establish contact retention or task success.

Keep evidence, failures and uncertainty in your working notes. Scope part IDs,
coordinates and plans to their episode. Transfer procedural lessons with their
support and limitations; distinguish supplied tool instructions from discoveries.

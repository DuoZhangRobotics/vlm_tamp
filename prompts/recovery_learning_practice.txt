Carry out the environment's language instruction to its full intended extent.
Read tools.txt and the supplied experience before acting. Use the simulator's
task_success as the benchmark outcome. The previous method is a starting
hypothesis, not a mandatory contact strategy.

This is adaptive practice in one continuous learning context. Begin with five
attempts; extend in blocks through request_attempts if needed, up to twenty.
Each attempt has 600 control steps and 30 minutes. A reset restores the scene,
while your context and notes persist. Within an attempt, keep investigating
until native success or an actual budget limit. No reset retry or voluntary
early finish is allowed while the current episode has budget. Runtime failures
are separate from physical failures. Do not waste actions merely to consume steps.

Use fresh images, depth and robot state, and obtain new plans after every reset.
You may write analysis code inside this workspace. Implementation source, old
project reports, other cases and private simulator state remain unavailable.
No external model API calls are allowed.

Manipulation may use several stages and different contact modes. Grasping,
releasing, regrasping, pushing, pulling and sliding are available choices when
supported by observations and the tools. Choose the surface, direction,
orientation and sequence yourself. Reobserve progress instead of assuming an
object followed planned or measured hand motion.

SUCCESS FIRST: seek a method that completes the task. After success, reproduce
it from a fresh reset. Two consecutive native successes establish a provisional
baseline. Only then consider fewer tool calls or longer useful trajectories.
Efficiency is optional in this recovery study and must not replace a more
reliable method. Complete at least five attempts; afterward you may stop with a
repeatable method and an explanation of remaining uncertainty. Without such
repeatability, request more attempts up to the hard cap. Do not ask the human for
a strategy or claim generalization from repeated success in this scene.

Before each attempt, write practice/attemptNN/plan.json with your hypothesis,
planned change or repeatability check, and predicted observations. Afterward,
save findings.txt and guidelines.json in that attempt's directory. Preserve
earlier notes. The first simulator success ends an episode immediately; no
retreat, release, return-home motion or extra verification is required.

Maintain root-level guidelines.json as a JSON object with a nonempty principles
list. Each principle should explain applicability, observable failure signals,
a conditional action or method, the evidence from numbered attempts, and limits.
Distinguish supplied ideas, your tested findings and unresolved hypotheses.
Record a method's failures as well as successes. A failed plan is not proof
that no feasible solution exists.

The transferable guidelines must contain no historical pixel boxes, world
coordinates, joint vectors, TCP poses, raw evidence paths or executable replay
scripts. Describe how to estimate geometry anew. Relative tool settings may be
retained with their observed scope and uncertainty. Put raw numeric evidence in
findings or per-attempt records, not in transferable guidelines.

At the end write practice-summary.json, including every attempt, native
outcomes, best_supported_method, remaining_tradeoffs and stop_reason. Keep
findings.txt and completion.json candid and consistent with tool feedback.
